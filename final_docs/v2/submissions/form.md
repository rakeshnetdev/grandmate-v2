# Project Submission Form — GrandMate

---

### What is the title of your project?

**GrandMate — a chess coaching companion that finds your habits, not just your blunders**

---

### Write a succinct 1-sentence description of the problem

Chess engines tell a player *what* went wrong in one game, but nothing tells them *which of
their mistakes are habits* — so players get a verdict on a single game instead of a
diagnosis of how they actually play.

---

### Write 1-2 paragraphs on why this is a problem for your specific user

Our primary user is the self-directed club player. They finish a game, run the engine, and
see `-2.4` at move 23 — and learn almost nothing they can act on. The engine's output is
per-move and per-game by construction: it can say a move lost material, but it cannot say
"this is the fourth time this month you've castled into a weakened king position," or "you
score 68% with White in the Ruy Lopez and 31% with Black in the French." Playing platforms
optimise for the game you just played; nobody optimises for the pattern across your last
sixty. The player is left doing the hardest part of coaching — pattern recognition across
their own history — by memory and intuition, which is exactly what they are least equipped
to do about their own blind spots.

A human coach solves this, but a coach reviewing thirty games by hand is expensive and
slow, and the two other people who need this information are served even worse. A coach
with several students needs fast per-student preparation before a lesson, and currently has
to do the cross-game synthesis manually for each one. A junior player needs feedback they
can actually read: raw centipawn losses and engine notation are not feedback for an
eleven-year-old, they are noise. Today all three audiences get the same undifferentiated
engine output — and when a tool does try to explain in natural language, it is a general
LLM that will confidently invent a variation that was never played, which is worse than no
explanation at all because a learner cannot tell the difference.

---

### Write 1-2 paragraphs on your proposed solution. How will it look and feel to the user?

GrandMate is a companion layer over the platforms people already play on, built as two
deliberately separated halves: a **deterministic chess core** that must be reproducible and
boring, and an **agentic RAG layer** that must be grounded, observable, and continuously
evaluated. The core computes what is true — Stockfish evaluation at a tiered depth policy,
five-way move classification, opening identification by position rather than move order,
and twenty tactical-motif and strategic-theme detectors — and aggregates it across windows
of the last 10, 30, or 60 games into recurring weaknesses, opening-family performance, and
progress deltas. The agent layer never asserts chess truth on its own: every explanation is
checked, before it reaches the reader, against the deterministic record it claims to
describe. If a generated answer cites a move, evaluation, variation, or opening that isn't
in that record, it is rejected, retried once, and then replaced by a deterministic
facts-only response. The user is never shown an ungrounded claim, and never shown an error
either.

In use it feels like this. You paste a PGN — or upload a season's worth — and within a
second the game is parsed, deduplicated, replayed move by move, and its opening named;
seconds later the engine analysis and pattern tags land, and the game page shows accuracy,
per-move classifications, and the tactical and strategic moments a coach would actually
flag. Your dashboard turns that into development insight: what you keep getting wrong,
which openings work for you, how White differs from Black — with a sample-size guard that
refuses to call two games a trend. A persona switcher renders that same set of facts three
ways — direct and second-person for a self-learner, technical and lesson-shaped for a
coach, short and centipawn-free for a child — with an automated fidelity check asserting
the facts stay identical across all three. And you can just ask: "what was my opening
here?", "what do I keep getting wrong?" — a tool-calling agent decides for itself whether
to retrieve your game's analysis, your profile aggregate, or the knowledge corpus, and
answers with citations you can check. It remembers, across sessions, that you prefer short
answers and that you're working on your time trouble — and shows you exactly what it
remembers on a page where you can delete any of it.

### Please provide a 1-2 sentence description of your project.

*(Used as the project description on YouTube and ROS.)*

GrandMate is a chess coaching companion that analyses your games with Stockfish, finds the
mistakes you make *repeatedly* rather than just the one you made on move 23, and explains
them differently depending on whether you're a self-taught club player, a coach preparing a
lesson, or a kid. Every explanation is generated by a tool-calling RAG agent and then
verified against the deterministic analysis before you see it — so it can't invent a move
you never played.

## Supporting facts, if the form or the reviewers ask for evidence

Everything below is verified against the running system, not aspirational.

| Claim | Evidence |
|-------|----------|
| Parsing reliability | 99.991% across a real 10,594-game corpus; the single failure is a zero-move forfeit that ingestion rejects earlier anyway |
| Opening detection | 0 no-matches across a 150-game real corpus; 79.3% exact ECO match, matched on position (EPD) so transpositions resolve correctly |
| Motif detector precision/recall | 20/20 recall, 0/10 false positives against real, independently tagged Lichess puzzles (CC0) — external ground truth, which caught a genuine skewer-detector bug |
| Retrieval quality (RAGAS, real corpus, real embeddings) | Hybrid: context precision 0.936, recall 0.977, MRR 0.949 |
| Persona fidelity (real `gpt-4o-mini` run) | `fact_invariance_rate` **100%**, `kid_safety_rate` **100%** — both hard-gated |
| Chat grounding (real run) | `grounded_rate` **100%**, `intent_valid_rate` **100%** — structural guarantees, not judge estimates |
| Memory quality (real run) | Retention true-positive and true-negative both **100%**; cross-profile isolation and staleness resolution verified against real Postgres |
| Engine determinism | Identical classifications across independent runs at `ENGINE_THREADS=1`, verified directly |
| Test suite | 679 backend tests, 64 frontend tests, `mypy --strict` clean |
| Scope delivered | Phases 0–11 of a 19-phase plan, each with its own branch, PR, and signed-off phase report |

**Architecture in one line**: FastAPI + LangGraph + python-chess + Stockfish over Postgres
with pgvector; React 19 + Vite + Tailwind + shadcn/ui on the front end; multi-bucket
agentic RAG with hybrid dense + sparse retrieval and reciprocal rank fusion; `gpt-4o-mini`
behind a provider abstraction; RAGAS evaluation recorded per run.
